How it works

Pacenote runs on your Mac. Pace answers you in Slack, keeps a list of what people ask of you, and hands each task to Claude Code or Codex. What it does is kept in ~/.pacenote.

The loop

Work reaches you in Slack threads and gets done in Claude Code or Codex. Each keeps its own history, so you paste the thread again, explain it again, and hunt for where you stopped. Pacenote keeps each task's history together on your Mac, whichever tool did the work.

  1. AskedA Slack thread and its link
  2. On your listCollected into Todo, with a summary
  3. Picked upWith the thread and how related work ended
  4. Worked onYour sessions and the branch
  5. Wrapped upHow it ended, and a reply to send

Why the name: in rallying, the co-driver reads the pace notes so the driver can keep their eyes on the road. You drive. Pace reads the notes.

An answer, step by step

  1. You mention @Pace. The mention reaches the app on your Mac over Slack's Socket Mode, a connection your Mac opens, or through your team's hub.
  2. Pace checks that it is you, in a public channel, then reads the thread and its files.
  3. It runs claude or codex on your Mac with read-only access to one folder, or in a Linux VM thrown away after the answer.
  4. It strips its own secrets from the answer, replies in the thread, and records every step.

On your Mac or in a sandbox

Pace runs the claude or codex CLI you are signed in to. On your Mac, the CLI gets read-only access to one folder you choose, never your credential folders.

In the built-in sandbox, each answer runs in its own Linux VM, started with Apple's Containerization framework. The VM has no network device of its own, reaches only the domains you allow through a proxy in Pace, and is thrown away after the answer. There, diagrams and charts come back as images, and Pace can add, list or close your tasks. The sandbox needs macOS 15 or later.

Your Todo

Press Collect now and Claude Code reads Slack through your claude.ai Slack connector, read-only. You get a list of what people asked of you, by your @mention, the other names people call you, or a user group that means you, and of what you said you would do.

Anything it is unsure about waits under Maybe, and no message becomes a task twice. Collecting is off until you turn it on.

Handing a task to Claude Code or Codex

Continue in Claude Code or Codex opens a terminal session with the task, the thread, what Pace found there, and how related work ended last time, even when that work was done in the other tool.

A session you start yourself in a repository is told its open tasks from the start, and Todo shows the work as it happens. When it is done, the task closes with a note on how it ended and, if someone is waiting, a reply for you to send.

Teammates and the hub

Pace answers only you. Anyone else who mentions it gets a note only they can see. Teammates each run their own Pace, on their own Mac and login.

A team can share one Slack app through a Pacenote hub. The hub sends each mention to the desktop of the person who wrote it, where Pace answers with that person's login, and the Slack tokens stay on the hub.

That is the difference from an assistant a company adds to Slack: that one is set up by an admin and runs under the company's account. Pace is one you set up yourself, on your Mac, with your own plan.

Also in Pacenote

  • Recap your day, week or month

    A day as a standup, a week by area with what carries over, a month by where the work went. Copied as text, ready to post.

  • See the areas you work in

    Pacenote sorts your tasks and your Claude Code and Codex sessions into the areas you contribute to. You correct what it gets wrong.

  • Let your AI tools look things up

    Pacenote's MCP server lets Claude Code, Codex and other MCP clients read what Pace did and what is on your list. Off until you add it, in one click.
