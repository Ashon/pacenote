Recap shows what you did: a day as a standup, a week by area, a month by where it went, from your tasks and your own
commits, written up in a few sentences when you ask.

```sh
brew update && brew upgrade --cask pacenote
```

The upgrade quits the running app first and reopens it, once. Claude Code and Codex sessions already open keep the
previous Pacenote tools until they start again.

## Recap

- **In the sidebar, between Todo and You.** Day, Week and Month, with the period before and after one click away. Todo
  is what is ahead, Recap what is behind.
- **A day is a standup.** Done (tasks, and your commits by repository), Doing (open tasks worked on today) and Next
  (open tasks, due ones first). In the morning, yesterday's done comes first.
- **A week, by area.** Each area's done tasks and its feature and release commits, the rest of its commits as a count,
  what carries over, and its share of the week. A month adds which areas started or went quiet, and who asked you most.
- **Your commits, as they are.** Their subjects only, by your git email, on any branch, in your repositories' folders on
  this Mac; never a diff. A commit under a rewritten hash counts once.
- **Write it up, when you click.** Claude Code, with no tools and only the recap's own lines, sums it up in a few
  sentences, the commits summed into the work they were. It is kept with the period, and Write again replaces it.
- **Copy, never send.** As Markdown, in Slack's formatting, or the written sentences alone.
- **For your AI tools.** `my_recap` gives Claude Code and Codex the same record, and the `recap` prompt writes your
  standup or weekly update in the session.

An area's name in a recap opens it on the You screen, and an area's Lately on You opens its week.
