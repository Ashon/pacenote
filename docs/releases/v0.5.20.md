A collection now knows who you are in Slack: the names people call you and the user groups that mean you, so what is
asked of you is told apart from what only looks like it.

```sh
brew update && brew upgrade --cask pacenote
```

The upgrade quits the running app first and reopens it, once. Claude Code and Codex sessions already open keep the
previous Pacenote tools until they start again.

## Who you are in Slack

- **Your names.** The next collection looks up your own Slack profile with its Slack connector, and does again every 30
  days. Settings > Todo says who it read Slack as, and warns when that is not the account Pace answers.
- **The other names people call you.** A nickname, or your name in another language, under Settings > Todo > "Other
  names people call you": a request that calls you by one is yours, @mention or not.
- **Groups that mean you.** An ask of a user group listed under "Groups that mean you", such as an on-call group, is a
  task. The collection searches for your names and groups as well as your @mention.
- **Asked once.** A Maybe from any other group says who asked which group, and asks "Does @design mean you?". **It's
  me** adds the group to Settings and keeps its Maybes as tasks; **Not me** leaves later asks of that group out, unless
  they name you.

Pacenote keeps your member id, names and workspace in `tasks.json`, and still holds no Slack token for tasks.
