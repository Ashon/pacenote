Kubernetes connects in three steps in Settings > Tools: pick your clusters, give each one Pace's read-only account,
and see what Pace reads with.

```sh
brew update && brew upgrade --cask pacenote
```

## Kubernetes in three steps

- Open **Settings > Tools > Kubernetes**. Under its settings, the steps say where each cluster stands. The
  [guide](https://pacenote.dev/guide/kubernetes/) walks through them.
- **Pick your clusters** from your kubeconfig's contexts. Contexts EKS names by ARN work too.
- **Give each cluster a read-only account.** Pace reads each cluster as an account of its own, `pacenote-ro` in the
  `pacenote` namespace, never as you. Each cluster says whether it has one:
  - **Make it here**, where your kubectl may: Pacenote says what it adds, and makes it once you confirm.
  - **For your admin**, where it may not: the YAML to send whoever runs the cluster. It also lets you read the account's
    token, so your Pacenote can use the account once it is applied.
  - When kubectl has to sign in, or the cluster doesn't answer, the step says what to do, with **Check again** for
    after.
- **Connect Pace.** Save, and Pace builds its own kubeconfig when it starts: each cluster's address and CA from your
  kubeconfig, and the account's token. Your own credentials never go into it. The step then asks each cluster, with
  that token, what Pace may do, and shows **Reads**, **No secrets** and **No changes**, or a warning when the account
  can do more than read.
- A cluster left out because its account wasn't there yet joins on **Connect again**. There is no kubeconfig to
  rebuild by hand after changing the settings; **Rebuild kubeconfig** stays for a token changed in a cluster.

## Fixes

- A CA kept in a file in your kubeconfig now reaches Pace's kubeconfig too.
