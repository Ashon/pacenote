Overview opens with your day in three lines and counts what recap write-ups spend, Todo says plainly when Claude Code is
signed out, and the app comes back after an upgrade that took longer than Homebrew waits.

```sh
brew update && brew upgrade --cask pacenote
```

The upgrade quits the running app first; open Pacenote again if it does not come back on its own. From this version on,
an app that takes longer to quit than Homebrew waits opens the new version itself.

## Your day at a glance

- **A briefing on Overview.** Up to three lines from today's recap: what is next (who asked, when it is due, how many
  more are open), what you are on, and what got done today, or yesterday in the morning. Each line opens the task in
  Todo or today's recap. Nothing is written for it, so it spends no tokens, and on a quiet day it is not there.
- **Recap write-ups in tokens per day.** What each Write it up spends is kept, written again or not. Overview stacks it
  on top of each day's bar in a teal of its own, and By source lists Recaps with what each write-up took.

## Signed out, said plainly

- **Collect now names Claude Code.** Collecting runs Claude Code on this Mac whichever CLI answers your mentions. When
  its sign-in ran out or was revoked, Todo says so, and **Sign in to Claude Code** opens `claude auth login` in your
  terminal. Reply drafts and Pace's failure notes read the same wordings.

## Back after an upgrade

- **Pace no longer stays off after an upgrade.** A copy of the old version that macOS opened to deliver Homebrew's quit
  could be told to stop before Pace had started, and the stop was lost: Pace ran until it was cut off 30 seconds later,
  Homebrew stopped waiting, and nothing opened the new version. The stop now reaches Pace once it has started, the ops
  broker starts no VM once stopped, and an app that outwaited Homebrew opens the new version itself.
