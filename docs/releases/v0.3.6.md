Pace stays quiet in the thread while it works: a 👀 on your mention instead of a "Working on it..." message, and the
answer as a new reply that notifies you.

```sh
brew update && brew upgrade --cask pacenote
```

The upgrade quits the running app first (the bot gets up to 20 seconds to finish its requests, and the rest resume on
the next start) and reopens it. Config and run history are untouched.

## A 👀 instead of a message

- When you mention Pace, it reacts to your message with 👀, and adds ⏳ if the answer takes longer than 90 seconds.
  Nothing is posted in the thread until the answer, so people talking there are not interrupted.
- The answer comes as a new reply at the bottom of the thread, so Slack notifies you when it is ready. (Before, it was
  edited into the "Working on it..." message, which Slack does not notify about.) The reactions come off once it is posted.
- A failure is a short reply too, and a restart keeps the 👀 on your message while Pace picks the request up again.

## Add one scope to your Slack app

Pace needs the `reactions:write` scope for this. Until the app has it, Pace works as before, posting "Working on it..."
and editing it into the answer, and the status bar shows "1 check issue" saying the scope is missing.

1. Open [api.slack.com/apps](https://api.slack.com/apps) and choose your Pacenote app.
2. OAuth & Permissions > Scopes > Bot Token Scopes > Add an OAuth Scope: `reactions:write`.
3. Choose "Reinstall to Workspace" in the banner at the top of the page, and allow it.

Pace picks the scope up with the next mention, without a restart, and the check issue goes away once a reaction works.
If Slack shows a new bot token after reinstalling, save it in Settings > Messengers > Slack.

With a team hub, the hub's admin adds the scope to the hub's Slack app and updates the hub, which now lets each desktop
react to the mentions routed to it.
